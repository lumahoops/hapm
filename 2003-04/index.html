HAPM

Held-Out Adjusted Plus-Minus ? HAPM measures how many points per 100 possessions a player's stints produced above or below expectation. The other nine players in each of his stints are rated without any of his possessions. Their ratings give the margin the stint would produce with an average player in his place, and his HAPM is the actual margin minus that. Players with few possessions are pulled toward a typical value for their playing time.

0 is the average rated player. Rotation players average about +1.3. Each cell shows the value and its percentile. Compare players Data on GitHub
▸ What is HAPM?

HAPM (held-out adjusted plus-minus) is a player's value in points per 100 possessions. Whereas RAPM solves for all 10 players at once and splits each stint's result among them, HAPM holds the player out, estimates each of his stints from the other nine players, and credits him with the actualized difference. 0 is an average player; rotation players average about +1.3.

▸ How HAPM works

HAPM is a player's net impact per 100 possessions, measured against an expectation built without him. The expectation comes from a ridge regression on every stint the measured player did not play, excluding his possessions from the entire regression, including the priors from each player's three previous seasons. His raw value is the possession-weighted mean of actual minus expected margin over his stints, with 0 in his place. That value is shrunk toward a playing-time center by its reliability.

1. Pick the player, rate the other nineTeammates and opponents are rated from every stint where the chosen player did not play, starting from their three previous seasons.
→
2. Estimate each stintThose ratings give the expected margin for each of his stints, with an average player in his place.
→
3. Actual minus expectedThe chosen player's HAPM is what his stints produced above or below that estimate, per 100 possessions.

Versions

Each version uses a different number of seasons of the player's own stints, with newer seasons counting more. The 3 means the other nine are rated with their three previous seasons.
