▸ What is HAPM?

HAPM (held-out adjusted plus-minus) is a player's value in points per 100 possessions. Whereas RAPM solves for all 10 players at once and splits each stint's result among them, HAPM holds the player out, estimates each of his stints from the other nine players, and credits him with the actualized difference. 0 is an average player; rotation players average about +1.3.

▸ How HAPM works

HAPM is a player's net impact per 100 possessions, measured against an expectation built without him. The expectation comes from a ridge regression on every stint the measured player did not play, excluding his possessions from the entire regression, including the priors from each player's three previous seasons. His raw value is the possession-weighted mean of actual minus expected margin over his stints, with 0 in his place. That value is shrunk toward a playing-time center by its reliability.

HAPM credits each player with the gap between those expectations and what happened. Versions

HAPM time decay uses every season of the player's own stints, newer seasons counting more: a season's weight halves every 1.5 seasons. HAPM 2-year uses this season and last season, this season counting twice as much. HAPM 1-year uses this season only. All three are pulled toward a typical value for the player's playing time; during the season in progress, playing time is read at his full-season pace. Par is this season's actual minus expected margin with no pull, so it swings the most; the ± under each Par value is one standard error (about two-thirds of the time the true value is within it). Every version rates the other nine with their three previous seasons.
